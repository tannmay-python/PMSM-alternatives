What replaces the permanent magnet motor?

01

The answer depends on what the motor has to do.

Permanent magnet synchronous motors became the usual choice for electric vehicles because they pack high torque and high efficiency into a small volume. Their magnets also tie a critical component to a concentrated supply chain.

No alternative wins every comparison. Wound-field motors trade magnets for rotor current. Induction motors accept rotor heat. Reluctance machines ask more of control software and acoustic design. Ferrite magnets are easier to source but weaker. The useful question is where each trade belongs.

Reading rule

Peak efficiency is one point. Range, cooling, cost and reliability depend on the whole operating map.

Prerequisite

A motor is a chase between two magnetic fields.

The stator creates a rotating field. The rotor follows it. Motor designs differ mainly in how the rotor gets its own field, and in what that choice costs.

Supply concentration, 2024

The exposure grows after the ore leaves the ground.

The International Energy Agency estimates that China accounted for 60 percent of mined magnet rare earths, 91 percent of separation and refining, and 94 percent of sintered permanent magnet production in 2024.

A useful correction

Some labels describe geometry or implementation, not a new branch of motor physics.

Axial flux describes the direction of magnetic flux and the shape of the machine. It can use neodymium, ferrite or an excited rotor.

Virtual magnet is a commercial label for an externally excited field created with coils, contactless power transfer and control software.

Material balance

Rare-earth-free does not mean material-free.

Removing neodymium can increase copper, electrical steel, cooling hardware or semiconductor demand. Ferrite may need more active material or a different geometry. A credible comparison counts mass, manufacturing, repair and recycling alongside motor efficiency.

    How to read the supplied report

    Holds up

    The engineering field is broader than permanent magnets.

    Wound-field, reluctance, induction and ferrite designs are all serious routes. Several are already deployed. Better control electronics and cooling are improving their usable envelope.

    Needs qualification

    Most headline figures come from the companies developing the products.

    Power density, range gain, cost reduction and production dates should be read as reported claims unless an independent test or a production vehicle supports them.

    Was missing

    Induction motors and industrial SynRM are important reference cases.

    Tesla uses induction alongside permanent magnets. ABB sells high-efficiency SynRM at industrial scale. Both show that the answer changes with the duty cycle and packaging constraints.

    Runs in parallel

    Substitution is one part of supply resilience.

    Recycling, lower-magnet designs, diversified refining and stockpiles can reduce exposure without removing every permanent magnet. Motor choice and material strategy should be evaluated together.

    There will be no single post-PMSM motor.

    The likely outcome is a portfolio. Wound-field machines already serve vehicles that can accept their rotor system. Induction remains useful where robust magnet-free torque matters. Reluctance fits industrial duty and may move further into traction as control and noise improve. Ferrite and iron nitride try to keep the permanent-magnet advantages with a different material base.

    The choice should follow the operating map, supply risk and evidence level. A motor that is easy to announce can still be difficult to cool, manufacture and qualify at scale.

    Sources and method

    Primary and official sources were preferred. Company performance figures remain attributed to the company. The loss and ranking models are teaching aids, not measured product comparisons.
